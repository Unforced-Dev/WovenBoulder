Key Topics

Oath of Office: New and Reappointed Commissioners Speaker sworn in for a reappointed 5-year term. Anna Pavlenko sworn in for a new 1-year term. Commissioner introductions: JH (Haiti-born, Boulder 16+ years, auto mechanic, 2 kids, joined HRC during pandemic); Anna (14 years in Boulder, science background, son helps with video/YouTube).

Boulder Junction Multi-Board Working Group City forming a cross-board liaison group to work on Boulder Junction Phase 2 — a transit hub redevelopment between Folsom/30th and Pearl/Baseline, east of downtown. Phase 2 focuses on mixed use, green space, public areas, and housing. Anna nominated to represent HRC; staff to follow up with meeting schedule. This is a first-of-its-kind multi-board collaborative approach for Boulder.

Protected Classes / Unhoused Status (March Meeting Follow-Up) Follow-up to a March 2023 panel discussion on whether unhoused people should be added as a protected class under Boulder's human rights ordinance (which covers housing, employment, and public accommodations for protected classes including race, gender identity, age, etc.). Staff explained the required process: any HRC interest must first be communicated to City Council via a letter or memo (typically summer/fall), to ensure Council is aligned before staff time is invested. All commissioners expressed interest in exploring further. Staff to share March recording with Anna and follow up on process.

Police Oversight Panel Crisis — Lisa Sweeney-Moran Christine provided a detailed update on fast-moving developments. Two new POP members were recently appointed, including Lisa Sweeney-Moran (nominated by community groups including the NAACP at City Council's request). Citizens filed letters of complaint about the appointment process. City Council appointed a special counsel (attorney) to investigate. That attorney recommended Sweeney-Moran resign, citing bias against police. Sweeney-Moran refused to resign; her attorney sent a letter to City Council that same day arguing her removal would be illegal.

JH shared his own experience: in February 2023 an officer followed him to his bank, then came to his auto shop the next day and accused him of depositing $70,000 in someone else's account. After months of pursuing complaints, the Boulder POP investigation yielded no accountability and cost him $7,000 in legal fees. He argued the HRC should support the POP's independence and that someone like Sweeney-Moran gives community members without power a voice.

Commissioners agreed: (a) POP members are not expected to be police cheerleaders; (b) Sweeney-Moran was nominated through a legitimate community process; (c) removing her based on opinions sets a dangerous precedent. Christine proposed and commissioners agreed to write a letter to City Council defending POP independence. City Council was scheduled to discuss the matter two days later (Thursday). Christine to draft letter within 2 weeks; staff to advise on process channel.

  • BVSD Youth Equity Council: Speaker organized a panel with the district’s 50+ member Youth Equity Council at her children’s school — middle/high school students from marginalized groups sharing experiences growing up in Boulder.

Human Relations Fund Transition The HRF grant-making function is formally moving out of HRC’s responsibility. Speaker agreed to serve on the new staff-run grant review committee. Staff to update the HRC manual to remove the HRF section. Organizations have already been notified.

Boards & Commissions Structure Review City manager Nuria requested an external consultant review for all boards and commissions. No timeline yet. Christine provided context: informal discussion 1.5 years earlier about whether a 5-person HRC with 5-year terms made sense.
