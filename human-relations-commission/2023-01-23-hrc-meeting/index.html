The January 2023 HRC meeting was dominated by an extended discussion of the Police Oversight Panel (POP) crisis, with City Equity Officer Amy Kane presenting the history and current status of the panel's near-collapse. Commissioner JH shared two separate traumatic personal experiences with Boulder police officers — including Officer Tally drawing a weapon on him while off-duty and out of uniform, and Officer Smiley falsely claiming JH stole his own car then summoning seven police cars — with both complaints resulting in "bogus reports" with conveniently missing audio. Chair Lindsay announced she would be stepping down after five years, leaving two HRC seats open for recruitment (deadline January 30). Staff provided updates on ARPA funds and the Guaranteed Income Project, City Council 2023 priorities, and transition of the Human Relations Fund to the city's investment team.

Key Topics

Police Oversight Panel Crisis Amy Kane (City Equity Officer) presented the full POP history: the 2019 Zayd Atkinson incident at Naropa sparked community outcry, leading to a task force and ordinance creating a 9-member POP plus 3 alternates. First IPM Joey LaPorty left for Los Angeles in September 2022; two final candidates were being vetted through a multi-panel interview process. A code of compliance complaint — filed by a Texas attorney just before City Council was to vote on new panel members — halted the appointment. A special meeting was set for Thursday to resolve the complaint and vote. A quorum crisis loomed: the panel needs six or more seated members by February 8 or it cannot operate, and two members scheduled to term off declined to stay on. Amy Kane acknowledged that HRC had historically not been consulted on policing — "a huge miss" — and committed to involving HRC in the next ordinance revision. Christine and Lindsay both expressed frustration at the politicization of the process; Lindsay described it as being "tripped up on technicalities because of people's political objectives."

Commissioner JH's Police Experiences JH shared in a public forum two serious incidents: (1) Officer Tally, out of uniform and not in a police car, put a gun to JH's head; a subsequent complaint investigation produced a report with missing audio and no accountability — the city later attempted to name a post office after Tally; (2) Officer Smiley pulled JH over, falsely claimed JH stole his own car, called seven police cars, and a complaint investigation yielded another missing-audio "bogus report." Smiley was later fired; JH believed it was partly due to his complaint. JH articulated a need for oversight that means something real — not for himself, but for his children.

HRC Recruitment Lindsay reached her five-year term limit and announced her departure. The Speaker was reapplying. Application deadline was January 30; staff interviews to begin February 13. Staff noted that of the 60 applicants not selected for the Guaranteed Income task force, many were directed to HRC and other board openings.

ARPA and Guaranteed Income Project The city received $20M in ARPA (American Rescue Plan Act) funds to be spent by end of 2026. Three tranches approved; Tranche 3 final City Council approval expected March 2. The Guaranteed Income Project — direct cash assistance with no strings attached for 1–2 years — had a 9-member community task force convened, all with lived experience with low income. Design decisions still to be made: monthly amount, target populations, application process, program duration. Consultant: Impact Charitable. Task force recommendations memo due to City Council for the March 12 meeting. Lindsay offered strong praise for the project, noting the evidence base for unconditional cash transfers.

City Council Priorities and HRC Role Staff shared Council's 2023 two-year priority list: ADU update, inclusionary housing, zoning for affordable housing, middle-income housing, day services center for homeless individuals, transportation core network, transit village area plan, and elections issues. City Council retreat set for February 23–24 (public can attend or watch). Staff acknowledged the challenge: HRC's engagement with these priorities depends on Council initiating contact, though commissioners can also signal interest.

HRC Departmental Move Staff are in active conversations about moving HRC from the Housing and Human Services Department to the City Manager's Office — to better reflect HRC's citywide role and tighten communication on cross-cutting issues. Goal: complete the move before new commissioners join.

Human Relations Fund Transition Management transitioning from Ingrid to Julia Daly (Human Investments Manager) and Marquesia Kehagen (grants specialist). New online application to be available by end of March, covering events from June–December 2023. Few events likely to be funded before summer while the transition is completed.

Golden West Senior Housing Lindsay raised Golden West's announced closure of its assisted living facility as an urgent issue for affordable senior housing. Staff to arrange a presentation — possibly from Older Adult Services or the county Area Agency on Aging — for an upcoming meeting.
