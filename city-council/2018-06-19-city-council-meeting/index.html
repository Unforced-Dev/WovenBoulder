Meeting Overview

Opened with two significant declarations: Lynn Mead, who donated her home to the city's Housing Legacy Program, and Karen Rondeau, the Human Services Director retiring after 22 years of service. The meeting then proceeded to extended open public comment covering renewable energy, the Museum of Boulder, campaign finance, South Boulder flood mitigation, municipal utility costs, transportation safety, sugar-sweetened beverage tax revenue, and the affordable housing crisis. Transcript cuts off approximately 43 minutes in, during open comment.

Key Items

Declaration — Lynn Mead Housing Legacy Day (June 22, 2018)

  • Second person to donate her home under the Housing Legacy Program for affordable homeownership
  • Originally purchased her home in 1996 when affordable as an educator; wishes to enable others in modest careers to own homes
  • Passed with unanimous standing ovation

Declaration — Karen Rondeau Day (June 19, 2018)

  • 22 years of city service beginning in 1996; roles included Manager of Children Youth and Families, Co-Director, Housing Director, Director of Housing and Human Services
  • Major accomplishments: Social Sustainability Plan, Early Childhood Task Force (now Early Childhood Council of Boulder County), child care subsidy innovation, homelessness strategy, Health Equity Fund (soda tax revenue), Substance Education and Awareness Fund (marijuana revenue), living wage policy expansion, Immigration Advisory Committee, Human Rights Ordinance work, failure-to-pay-wages ordinance
  • Passed with unanimous standing ovation

Open Comment Highlights

  • Renewable Energy: Leslie Quest — 8,600 U.S. temperature records set in May 2018; Xcel received ~50,000 MW of wind/solar/storage bids; Boulder solar capacity >20 MW (goal: 100 MW); Xcel's $2B fossil fuel assets create renewable adoption barriers
  • Museum of Boulder: Grand opening May 19 (2,500 attendees); 1,000+ visitors since; Leonardo da Vinci exhibit through July; permanent Boulder Experience Gallery opening November 19, 2018
  • Campaign Finance/Electronic Petitioning: Barbara Turner — Denver's U.Sign system operates on iPad (not internet-connected); working group unanimously supports electronic petitioning to reduce City Clerk verification burden
  • South Boulder Flood Mitigation: Multiple Frazer Meadows residents — five years post-2013 flood with no construction started; current plans show 5+ more years to completion; elderly residents' safety at risk
  • Municipal Utility Costs: Patrick Murphy — true 2018 project cost over $27M (acknowledged $17M + $10M underground transmission loss); project not operational as originally scheduled; go/no-go vote now deferred to 2020; Xcel will have 10x Boulder's renewable equivalent by year-end 2018
  • Sugar-Sweetened Beverage Tax (Ordinance 8267): Authorizes city to retain revenues exceeding original 2016 voter projections (otherwise returned to distributors under TABOR); 19 organizations have received Health Equity Fund grants; ballot measure to be placed on November ballot
  • Affordable Housing: Mark Hillman — 40+ year housing crisis; criticized council for advocating affordable housing while blocking development; argued Boulder has chosen housing scarcity as deliberate policy

Outcomes and Follow-Up

  1. June 22 declared Lynn Mead Housing Legacy Day; June 19 declared Karen Rondeau Day
  2. Ordinance 8267 (soda tax excess revenue retention) to go to November ballot; city to clarify funds already collected and will follow original measure intent
  3. Electronic petitioning technical work group tasked to determine feasibility
  4. South Boulder flood mitigation to be expedited; council committed to accelerated process
  5. Museum of Boulder permanent gallery opening November 19, 2018
