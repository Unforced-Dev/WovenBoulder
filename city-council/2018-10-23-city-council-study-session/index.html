Date: October 23, 2018 Type: Study Session

Meeting Overview

Study session focused on funding Boulder's climate and energy goals. Held just before the 2018 elections, the session acknowledged the October 2018 IPCC report (10–12 years to avoid catastrophic climate change), discussed insufficient current funding to meet climate goals, and identified interim actions to address a $4.4M annual funding gap.

Key Items

Ballot Measure Positions

  • Opposed: Amendment 74 (local governments liable for property value diminishment from regulations); Proposition 109 (transportation bonds without funding)
  • Supported: Proposition 110 (statewide sales tax for transportation); Proposition 112 (larger setbacks for oil and gas); Measure 7G (mill levy for Urban Drainage and Flood Control District)

Climate Action Tax (CAP Tax) Performance

  • Adopted 2007 for Kyoto Protocol targets (7% GHG reduction below 1990 levels)
  • Annual revenue: $1.75M
  • Avoided/reduced emissions: 500,000+ metric tons CO₂ since inception
  • Cost effectiveness: $35/metric ton (vs. California utility programs at $70+/metric ton)
  • Leverage ratio: $4+ in private investment for every $1 in CAP tax spending
  • Direct documented reductions: ~125,000 metric tons

Community Emissions Progress

  • 16% reduction in community emissions (2017 GHG inventory) — surpasses 2020 goal of 15%
  • Achieved ahead of schedule due to cleaner electricity grid
  • GDP up 40% since baseline while energy consumption flat or declining
  • Landfill diversion rate: 51%; 2020 goal: 50% emissions reduction; 2050 goal: 80%
  • Challenge: need to double emissions savings from past 13 years within next 12 years

Funding Gap

  • Unfunded needs: $4.4M annually (2020–2025)
  • Current CAP tax only taxes electricity; natural gas, gasoline, and vehicles not included
  • Misalignment problematic because city wants to electrify buildings and transportation

Recommended Actions

  • Advance further analysis on vehicle registration efficiency fee as interim funding mechanism
  • Delay evaluation of adjusting electricity and natural gas taxes until after 2020 municipal election
  • Local electric utility revenue expected by earliest 2024

Unfunded Program Areas

  • EV charging infrastructure (DC fast charging for multifamily residents)
  • Transit electrification
  • Solar and efficiency rebates (currently oversubscribed, run out mid-year)
  • Building electrification (especially multifamily)
  • Energy equity and resilience through trees; food waste and food choice education; ecosystem and resource consumption programs

Climate Science Context

  • IPCC recommended carbon pricing $135–1,550/metric ton by 2030 for 1.5°C target
  • Switzerland and Sweden: ~$137/metric ton; Boulder's CAP tax significantly lower
  • Nearly 40 countries and 24 sub-national regions have carbon pricing mechanisms

Outcomes and Follow-Up

  1. Proceed with analysis of vehicle registration efficiency fee as interim funding mechanism
  2. Natural gas and electricity tax adjustments deferred to after 2020 municipal election
  3. January 2019 study session: biennial climate commitment update with ecosystem and resources focus
  4. December 16: Scope food/food-waste projects with CU Boulder MEM graduate program
  5. 2019: Begin community education/awareness campaign on food emissions
  6. 2017 GHG inventory report completion expected within ~1 month
