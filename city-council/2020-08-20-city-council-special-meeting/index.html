Date: August 20, 2020 Type: Special Meeting

Meeting Overview

Special meeting to consider placing the Xcel Energy franchise agreement on the November ballot, including four related ordinances on utility occupation taxes and energy goals. The council reviewed detailed emissions targets, termination rights, buyout options, a multi-project Energy Partnership Agreement, and the status of the Local Power Initiative (condemnation action paused pending franchise resolution).

Key Items

Ordinances Under Consideration

  • Ordinance 4110: Franchise agreement (first reading)
  • Ordinance 4116: Repurpose utility occupation tax from bill assistance to general fund/summer projects
  • Ordinance 4117: Extend utility occupation tax provisions through 2027
  • Ordinance 4124: Additional utility occupation tax measure
  • All four advancing to second reading contingent on November ballot approval

Xcel Energy Franchise Agreement

  • Previous proposal rejected by city in 2017
  • Proposed $33 million Xcel investment over first five years
  • Damages cap: $200 million (includes acquisition/distribution assets, two substations, going concern valued at $235M in prior proposal)
  • City buyout options: 2025, 2030, 2035, 2040, 2045 (or five-year intervals without restriction)
  • Termination rights without cause if Xcel misses GHG targets in 2023, 2025, or 2028
  • Xcel agreed to refrain from campaign contributions in 2020, 2021, and 2024–2025

Emissions Reduction Targets

  • Xcel statewide 2005 baseline: ~34 million tonnes CO2 equivalent
  • State requirement (HB 1261 / SB 236): 80% emissions reduction by 2030
  • Boulder city goal: 100% renewable electricity by 2030
  • Interim targets established for 2022, 2024, and 2027
  • Tracking: Climate Registry with third-party audit verification
  • Methane reporting: Distribution system begins 2021; all business segments begin 2022 (ONE Future Consortium)
  • Colorado Regulation 7: Oil and gas emissions reduction compliance begins 2021

Energy Partnership Agreement

  • Governance: Executive team (Xcel CO president + city/county leadership); oversight team (subject matter experts); advisory panel (up to 15 representatives from residential, commercial, industrial, university)
  • Evaluation criteria: equity commitments, accessibility to all customer classes, racial equity pathways, broad benefit distribution
  • Specific projects: Chautauqua micro-grid feasibility, solar on water storage tanks, energy center with micro-grid, hydrogen storage exploration, battery storage, streetlight control systems

Policy Changes

  • Legislative advocacy to eliminate 120% limitation on site-generated renewable energy
  • New tariff development for electric bus fleet conversion
  • Barriers removal for distributed generation

Local Power Initiative Status

  • 10 full-requirement offers received (100% renewable supply); 5 partial-requirement offers; 1 local battery storage offer
  • Pricing comparable to 2018 indicative pricing requests
  • Legal work paused pending resolution of second condemnation action
  • Condemnation case dismissal agreed to in exchange for franchise agreement cooperation

Outcomes and Follow-Up

  1. Four ordinances (4110, 4116, 4117, 4124) advancing to second reading; contingent on November ballot passage
  2. Xcel GHG interim targets for 2022, 2024, 2027 with city termination rights if missed
  3. Energy Partnership Agreement governance and project evaluation framework adopted
  4. Methane reporting: distribution system 2021; all segments 2022
  5. Feasibility studies for Chautauqua micro-grid, water tank solar, energy centers, hydrogen storage, streetlight controls
  6. Legislative advocacy to eliminate 120% site-generation limitation
  7. Electric bus fleet tariff development underway
  8. Local Power Initiative legal work paused; 15 renewable proposals under evaluation
