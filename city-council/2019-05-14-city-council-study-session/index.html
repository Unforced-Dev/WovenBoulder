Date: May 14, 2019 Type: Study Session

Meeting Overview

Study session presenting findings from a statistically valid survey of over 1,300 residents conducted as part of the Open Space Master Plan (OSMP) planning process. Results covered community preferences on trail management, wildlife management, chemical use, land acquisition priorities, and funding — informing the draft OSMP scheduled for release May 20, 2019.

Key Items

Survey Methodology

  • 1,300+ residents responded (23% response rate); 2,300 additional online responses
  • Target audience: Areas 1, 2, and 3 within the Boulder Valley Comprehensive Planning Area
  • Statistically valid design confirmed by Process Committee
  • Targeted outreach: Youth Options Advisory Board, Latino community, people with disabilities, agricultural producers

Focus Area Validation

  • 91% found Agriculture (newly added at Board recommendation) at least somewhat important
  • 98% supported Ecosystem Health and Resilience as a focus area
  • Strong community confirmation of all council-approved focus areas (from July 2018)

Recreation and Trail Management

  • Parking congestion and trail crowding key concerns, especially at Chautauqua and Encinas
  • 87% support for low or no-cost shuttles to trailheads
  • 75% prefer improving existing trails over building new ones
  • Education identified as most supported strategy for high-visitation areas
  • ~30% support charging for parking; even fewer support reservation systems

Wildlife and Habitat Management

  • 59% support lethal control for prey populations on/near irrigated farmland when other approaches unsuccessful (higher in Areas 2–3)
  • 89% support closing designated trails in sensitive habitat areas
  • 89% support extending trail requirements into sensitive habitat areas
  • 86% support temporary trail closures for wildlife/habitat protection

Chemical Management and Land Use

  • 44% support targeted synthetic herbicide use for invasive weeds; 56% opposed
  • ~75% prioritize conserving existing ecosystems over acquiring new lands
  • Community divided on temporal use separation (bikes on certain days, hiking on others)

Funding Priorities ($100 allocation exercise)

  1. Restoring degraded ecosystems and wildlife habitat
  2. Maintaining and improving trails and amenities
  3. Acquiring more open space
  • 92% of city residents support restoration of sales tax funding for open space (highest-polling result across all recent surveys)
  • Slight majority prefers perpetual funding over 10-year time limit

Outcomes and Follow-Up

  1. Draft Master Plan to be released May 20, with comment period through June 12; early comments (by June 3) summarized for June 11 study session
  2. Executive summary to be translated to Spanish
  3. Community drop-in hours: May 30 (Thursday) and June 1 (Saturday)
  4. Joint City Council and Open Space Board of Trustees study session: June 11 (3 hours)
  5. Brief questionnaire to council and Board members to identify priority discussion areas for June 11
  6. Final Draft Master Plan presentation to City Council: September 3, 2019
  7. Staff to develop refined funding priorities incorporating survey findings
